# Bridge3R auxiliary multi-cut and negative-control protocol

**Status:** pre-registered before any Bridge3R multi-cut inference is read.
**Scope:** supplementary evidence only; it does not alter the single-cut primary
result source or the fixed paper method.

## Purpose and scope

The primary paper protocol contains one hard viewpoint transition within one
physical capture.  This auxiliary protocol tests whether the same locked
transaction can be composed across two such transitions.  It is deliberately
limited to 150-frame, three-shot sequences from one synchronized Harmony4D
capture.  It does not support a claim about arbitrary edits, different scenes,
or unrestricted-duration video.

## Frozen source archives

The complete set consists of these four training archives, in this exact order:

1. `train/10_karate2.zip`
2. `train/11_karate3.zip`
3. `train/14_mma3.zip`
4. `train/15_mma4.zip`

Each archive is read from the immutable `Harmony4D.zip` outer archive.  For
each archive, the eligible capture is the first coordinate-valid capture in
the existing SHA-256 structural order used by the Harmony4D staging code.  No
network prediction, Bridge3R prediction, baseline output, or test metric is
consulted in this selection.

## Three-shot construction

For each selected capture, a 150-frame contiguous interval is divided into
three 50-frame shots.  The interval begins at the minimum synchronized frame
number.  Camera triplets are selected from calibration and GT-only visibility
audits by the deterministic implementation accompanying this document:

- every camera must pass the calibration reprojection audit and have at least
  1% visible GT vertices for every audited person;
- the two adjacent camera transitions must each have a nonzero baseline;
- among feasible ordered triples, select the lexicographically first triple
  after sorting by descending minimum adjacent rotation angle, descending
  minimum adjacent camera-centre baseline, and camera names;
- camera choice is therefore fixed before neural inference and independent of
  method outcomes.

The two emitted boundary indices are 50 and 100.  The manifest records the
entire frame and camera order, calibration-derived transition spans, archive
checksum, and selection rule.

## Evaluation and disclosure

For each of the four sequences, evaluate Strict Human3R and the one frozen
Bridge3R algorithm.  Report every sequence, each boundary separately, and the
macro mean of W-MPJPE, WA-MPJPE, ATE-Sim3, IDF1, coverage, and availability.
No sequence may be removed based on model performance.  If evaluator data are
unavailable, retain a reason-coded row rather than silently dropping it.

The same four captures additionally produce no-cut controls: one 150-frame
stream from a single selected camera with no view switch.  Their detector
output, no-cut bit-exact transaction result, and any false positives are
reported.  A detector false positive is an outcome, not an exclusion.

## Provenance boundaries

This is a real-image, same-scene viewpoint-switch construction from synchronized
captures.  It is not presented as a benchmark for cross-scene editing or as an
unconstrained edited-video dataset.  The resulting 3D renderings may be used
as a no-GT qualitative demonstration only when their camera, body, and identity
arrays are directly generated by the locked runtime.
