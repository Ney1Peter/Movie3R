Reconstructing human motion in a common world coordinate system from a
monocular video composed of multiple shots is challenging, as abrupt shot
transitions can cause drastic changes in viewpoint, visible scene content,
and human appearance. Existing online methods generally assume temporally
continuous input, while prior multi-shot approaches jointly process shots or
refine the observed sequence rather than producing recurrent estimates as
frames arrive. At a shot transition, recurrent history provides a reference
for recovering the inter-shot world relation, yet propagating the same state
through the new shot can induce time-varying camera drift, while
reinitialization alone loses that reference. To address this conflict, we present \method{}, an online framework
for streaming multi-person 4D reconstruction from multi-shot videos that
decouples inter-shot alignment from recurrent state propagation.
At each boundary, a history-conditioned path estimates the inter-shot
transform, while an independently reinitialized path supplies the state
propagated through the new shot. Geometry-based association establishes
cross-shot identities, and a shared transform registers cameras and humans to
the existing world frame.
Across three multi-person benchmarks, \method{} reduces the
equally weighted mean W-MPJPE by 9.5\% and improves mean IDF1 by 0.099 in
absolute terms over direct recurrent-state carry-over. On the predefined
extreme-viewpoint subset of EgoBody, \method{} reduces W-MPJPE by 29.9\%
while retaining frame-wise streaming inference without future frames or
per-video optimization.
